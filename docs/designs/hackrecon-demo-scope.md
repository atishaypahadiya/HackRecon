# Design: HackRecon 3–4 Day Demo (Replay + Missed-It Receipt)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-10-27
Branch: main
Repo: hackrecon (local, no remote)
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

At large hackathons like Cal Hacks, the event Slack has 100+ channels. Sponsors post API credits, internship links, prize criteria, and workshops, and their reps appear in sponsor channels. First-time hackers drown in it and miss credits, deadlines, and the chance to meet reps before day 1. Organizers build their own dashboards (e.g. Cal Hacks' Hive), and MLH resources are opt-in. Nothing works from the hacker's side at any event without organizer buy-in.

HackRecon is that hacker-side tool. The goal for the next 3–4 days is the strongest possible demo on real Cal Hacks workspace data. It's for personal use first, with an MLH follow-up afterward.

## What Makes This Cool

- **It extracts, it doesn't summarize.** Existing chat tools (Tal;dera, Podsumowywator, Slack's AI add-on) are admin-installed bots that turn a time window into prose. HackRecon turns the noise into typed items you can act on: a credit with a $ value, a deadline with a date, a rep with a company. And it runs from the hacker's own logged-in session.
- **The Missed-It Receipt.** Replay Cal Hacks week morning by morning. A money clock shows the open per-person credit value: it rises as sponsors post credits and drops as each deadline passes. The replay ends on a receipt built only from what was captured: "In C captured channels: $X in per-person credits across N offers, plus M unpriced offers, K dated deadlines, R reps at S sponsors." Missing out stops being a vague feeling and becomes a dollar figure, which makes a single strong slide for MLH.
- **The reps tab shows a relationship that can be warmed up.** Reps are grouped by company, each with a LinkedIn search link and a one-line opener that references their actual post. The user sends every message.

## Constraints

- Solo builder, 3–4 days.
- Hacker-side only: no organizer integration and no admin-installed bot.
- **No self-bots in the product.** Definition used in this doc: the product never sends requests to Slack beyond what the user's open web client makes in response to UI actions in the visible tab (scrolling, opening a channel, opening a profile card), and it never posts, reacts, or acts as the user. Automated scrolling in the visible tab, started by the user, falls within this rule. Calling Slack's API endpoints directly with the session token does not. Residual risk: automated scrolling may still conflict with the workspace's terms of service, so capture runs once, at a human-like pace, for personal use.
- No LinkedIn scraping: search URLs only. The user sends every message manually.
- Cal Hacks is over. Workspace access may be revoked at any time. If the workspace is on a free Slack plan, messages older than 90 days become hidden.
- **No backend of our own and no accounts.** Captured data stays on disk locally. Message text from the captured channels is sent to the Anthropic API for classification and opener drafting, and nothing else leaves the machine. All text is redacted before sending, removing @-mentions and known names (see "Redaction" under Item rules). Rep names are never sent: openers use a `{first_name}` placeholder that's filled in locally.
- No invented numbers anywhere in the UI.

## Premises (agreed)

1. The demo replays real Cal Hacks history ("what a first-timer would have seen each morning"). It isn't a live run. Capture happens first.
2. Capture is done by a minimal version of the real MV3 Chrome extension. It reads the rendered Slack web client (DOM) into JSON and is run once now. A one-time slackdump export **already ran** (2026-10-27 03:17, full workspace, 1,028 conversations, at `~/projects/hackrecon-data/calhacks`, outside the repo). It's the insurance copy and the ground truth for measuring capture quality. slackdump data is **only a comparison baseline**. It is never imported into the product's tables. Its only sanctioned uses are the compare step, the one-time `config/organizers.json` (A5, user direction) and the thread-gap counts in the receipt footnote (D4). No baseline message appears in the demo.
3. "New since last run" is simulated by a day slider over message timestamps. Daily scheduling is out of scope.
4. The board leads with one headline number. Credits and deadlines are the hero; jobs, workshops, and prizes are secondary rows.
5. Reps by company, with a LinkedIn search URL and a one-line opener, stays in at about half a day. It's the differentiator from organizer dashboards. Only sponsor reps count; organizers and hackers stay out of the people view.
6. Classification is a batch LLM script over the captured JSON (see Constraints for exactly what goes to the API).

## Cross-Model Perspective

An independent Claude subagent did a cold read; Codex wasn't installed. Its main points:
- **Coolest version:** the "Missed-It Receipt" plus a money clock. This was adopted. Its "~15% seen by a typical first-timer" figure was rejected as invented.
- **What excites the builder:** "AI makes it possible now where manual curation didn't work before." It also noted that validation so far comes from one conversation with one person.
- **50% of the way there:** slackdump covers capture. Classification, replay, reps, and the real extension are the other 50%.
- **Challenged premise #2 (Critical):** reading the rendered page is slow and loses messages. It proposed calling Slack's internal API from the extension instead. **Resolution:** that path is outside the no-self-bot boundary above, so it was rejected for the product. slackdump was adopted as a one-time comparison baseline.
- **Secondary risk:** "anyone posting in a sponsor channel = a rep" will pull in organizers and hackers asking questions. This is addressed by the reps rule threshold and allowlist below.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Capture + Board (minimal viable)
Capture once, classify in a batch, show a static board as of the end of the event. About 2 days. It's the fallback if the day-2 gate fails. On its own it undersells the idea: no "new since", no money moment.

### Approach B: Live Extension (ideal architecture)
Side panel, IndexedDB storage, capture on every visit, in-extension classification, a diff against the last run, and daily runs via `chrome.alarms`. Rejected for now because it's 7–10 days solo and there's no live event to demo it on. It's the post-demo target, and A's schema is designed so B adds scheduling and a diff, not a rewrite.

### Approach C: Replay + Missed-It Receipt (chosen)
Approach A's pipeline, plus a day slider with "new since yesterday" badges, a money clock, a reps tab, and a shareable receipt.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach C, built on A's pieces as B's foundation**, with these amendments from the user:

1. **No invented numbers.** The receipt shows only what's in the data. Anything without a stated per-person USD value is **"unpriced"**: it's listed with its stated amount text and never estimated or added to the dollar total.
2. **Schema shared with B.** Capture and classification write the same records B would store in IndexedDB (below).
3. **Hard gate at the end of day 2,** with a fallback defined for each failure mode (below).

### Config (hand-written on day 1)

- `config/event.json`: event timezone (`America/Los_Angeles`), the start and end of hacking as absolute instants, and the oldest visible message date recorded today.
- `config/sponsors.json`: channel → sponsor, kind and aliases (e.g. `spons-microsoft` → `Microsoft` with aliases `["Azure"]`). It's the **single channel list** (D11): the user's **locked 25** in capture order, plus the other 29 sponsor channels added with `"stretch": true`. The capture checklist, `runs.channels` and the compare step all read it. This drives the reps rule and normalizes `items.sponsor`.

### Schema

- `messages`: `id` (`<channel_id>:<ts>`), `channel_id`, `channel_name`, `ts`, `thread_ts?`, `author_name`, `author_title?`, `text`, `links[]`, `permalink`, `captured_at`, `run_id`
- `items` (zero or more per message): `message_id`, `item_index`, `kind` (credit | job | prize | workshop | deadline | other), `sponsor` (normalized via `sponsors.json`), `offer_key`, `amount_text`, `amount_value?`, `amount_unit` (USD | credits | tokens | hours | other), `amount_basis` (per_person | per_team | pool | up_to | unknown), `amount_usd_per_person?`, `deadline_iso?` (with an explicit offset), `deadline_status` (explicit | resolved_relative | unresolved | none), `urls[]`, `model`, `classified_at`
- `reps`: `rep_key` (`<sponsor>|<author_name>`), `sponsor`, `author_name`, `author_title?`, `sample_message_id`, `opener`, `confirmed` (bool), `model`, `generated_at`
- `runs`: `run_id`, `started_at`, `channels[]`, `message_count`, `source` (always `extension`)
- The slackdump baseline lives at `~/projects/hackrecon-data/calhacks`, outside the repo. The compare step reads it filtered to the channels in `sponsors.json`, and nothing else uses it.

`items` is stored separately from `messages`, so reclassifying never touches captured data.

### Item rules

- **Multiple items per message.** A post that bundles two credit programs plus a prize yields three items.
- **amount_usd_per_person** is set **only** when `amount_unit = USD` and `amount_basis = per_person`. Per-team amounts, pools, "up to $X", tokens, and hours are **unpriced** and appear in an "Unpriced offers" list with their `amount_text`.
- **Deadline linking across messages happens only through offer grouping.** If a reminder or follow-up ends up in the same offer group, its deadline applies to that offer (see aggregation below). A deadline posted in a message that isn't grouped with the offer is not linked to it, and the offer shows "no stated deadline". This is a documented limitation. The labeled set measures how often it happens, and the number is reported at the gate.
- **Sponsorless items** (from #announcements, prizes, jobs, or workshops, with no sponsor in `sponsors.json` and none named in the text) get `sponsor = "event"`. The `event` bucket goes through the URL merge, the deadline key, and the credit hand pass like any sponsor.
- **Offer grouping (dedup), cheapest honest version. There's no LLM grouping pass.** Grouping only matters for the receipt and money clock, and those ship only if the day-2 gate passes, so grouping moves off day 2 entirely.
  1. **URL merge** (deterministic, in the batch script): within each `sponsor + kind`, merge items that share any URL.
  2. **Deadline key** (deterministic): K counts distinct `(sponsor, deadline_iso rounded to the nearest 30 minutes)`. Reminders of the same deadline collapse automatically. So does a credit's deadline that's also posted as a separate `kind=deadline` item, so one real deadline is never counted twice.
  3. **Credit hand pass** (morning of day 4, ~45 min budget). The script writes `review/credits.csv`: one row per credit item after URL merge, sorted by sponsor then `ts`. In it, the human:
     - assigns `offer_key`, merging reminders and cross-posts
     - confirms `amount_usd_per_person` and `amount_basis`
     - links a deadline that was posted in a separate message
     - resolves `unresolved` deadlines
     
     Credit items are the only ones feeding N, M, and $, and with 117 credit-signal top-level messages in the locked 25 [measured: export] they're a short list. If the sheet has more than 100 rows, only rows with a stated amount get the full pass. The rest stay listed as unpriced, unmerged offers, which can overstate M but never the $ total.
  4. **K list scan** (day 4, ~10 min): look over the distinct deadline keys for false merges or splits.

  **Who owns the hand fixes (D7):** `review/credits.csv` is the only place they live. `offers.ts` applies it over the classifier's items, keyed by `message_id` + `item_index`, on every run, and `items.json` is never hand-edited. `hr sheet` appends rows for new credit items and never overwrites the human columns. If a row's key matches no item (e.g. after a reclassify), it's listed and `hr frames` refuses to build until it's fixed.

  **Row fingerprint (OV5, completes D7):** each sheet row also stores the item's `kind` and an `amount_text` fingerprint. If either no longer matches the item at that key (e.g. a reclassify reordered `[credit, prize]` to `[prize, credit]`), the row counts as orphaned and `hr frames` refuses to build.

  **Exclude column (OV6, required by "no invented numbers"):** the sheet has an `exclude` column for items that aren't offers (e.g. hacker questions like "do we only get 5 dollars of credit?"). Excluded items never count toward N, M, K or $. They're listed in the review output only.
  
  The classifier doesn't emit `offer_key`. It's assigned by steps 1 and 3.
- **What feeds the receipt:**
  - N = credit groups that are priced at the final D.
  - M = credit groups that are unpriced at the final D.
  - K = distinct deadline keys at the final D (step 2), of any kind.
  - Jobs, prizes, and workshops are grouped by URL merge only and don't feed any receipt sum.
- **Offer-level aggregation** (per `offer_key` group, evaluated at morning D):
  - `first_post` = the earliest item `ts` in the group.
  - **Priced at D:** the group has a priced item posted at or before D.
  - **Amount at D:** the `amount_usd_per_person` of the first priced item posted at or before D. An amount stated in a later post is never shown earlier.
  - **Deadline at D** = the most recent resolved `deadline_iso` among the group's items posted at or before D. An extension therefore applies from the morning it was posted.
  - If a later item in the group states a different amount, the conflict is flagged for the hand check.
  - **Deadline status at D:** `resolved` if a resolved deadline exists at D, otherwise `unresolved` if any item at D is unresolved, otherwise `none`.
- **Unresolved deadlines.** Before the demo, every priced credit offer whose deadline status is `unresolved` gets its deadline resolved by hand from the source post. Any that can't be resolved are **excluded from the money clock**, listed under "deadline unclear", and not counted in K.
- **The receipt and money clock sum distinct offers.** Each number links to **all** source permalinks in its group.
- **"K dated deadlines"** = the number of distinct deadline keys (step 2) among items posted at or before the final D. Unresolved deadlines have no `deadline_iso`, so they're never counted.
- **Redaction before any API call.**
  - **Name list:** the batch script builds a list from every `author_name` in the capture, including first names and display-name variants. **Before tokenizing (OV2):** it strips bracket tags (`[Figma]`, `[CAL HACKS]`) and `, Company` suffixes. It removes every sponsor name and alias in `sponsors.json`, plus a stopword/dictionary list (e.g. data, bright, long, grant, mark, park, sun, ai, cal, hacks). It matches full names, or capitalized tokens only. Measured collisions it must survive: poster names contain fetch ×10, microsoft ×9, bright ×5, data ×5, figma ×3, factory ×3 [measured: users.json].
  - **What's replaced:** Slack mentions (`<@U…>` tokens and rendered `@Name`) become `@person`, and any listed name becomes `[person]`.
  - **Openers:** generated from the sponsor name and the rep's redacted sample post, with a `{first_name}` placeholder filled in locally, so rep names are never sent.
  - **Residual risk:** names that never appear as a poster (e.g. in a signature) can slip through. The script logs the redaction count per batch.
- **Classifier cache (D9).** The cache key is `message_id` + sha256(redacted text + prompt text + JSON schema + model id), so any prompt, schema, model or redaction change re-classifies automatically. Each entry stores its prompt hash, and `hr gate` reports the hash it scored.
- **Classifier failures (D10).** An API error or an output that fails the schema is retried up to 2 times with backoff. After that, the message goes into `classify_errors.json` with the reason and isn't cached. A failed labeled positive counts as a gate miss, and `hr gate` prints the error count. `hr frames` refuses to build while errors exist unless run with `--allow-errors`, which adds "N messages not classified" to the receipt footnote.
- **Poster role (D17).** Classifier input includes `poster_role`: organizer, sponsor-side or other. It's a label computed from the A5 rules, never a name, and it's a hint, not a filter. The prompt rule is about the post's shape: from an `other` poster, questions and complaints are not offers, but announcement-shaped posts (stating an amount, code, link or how to claim) are classified normally. After the day-3 rep confirmations, `poster_role` is recomputed from the confirmed reps. The D9 cache key includes the input, so affected messages reclassify automatically before the day-4 hand pass.
- **Classifier input.** Each message goes in with its posted datetime in the event timezone, its channel name, its sponsor from `sponsors.json`, and the event schedule from `event.json`. That way relative deadlines ("tonight", "Sunday 11:59", "end of hacking") resolve to absolute instants. Deadlines it can't resolve get `deadline_iso = null` and `deadline_status = unresolved`, which the UI shows as a "deadline unclear" badge.

### Replay semantics

- **Timezone and frames (D16):** times are `America/Los_Angeles`. The frame list is computed in `offers.ts` from the data, never hand-written. It has a frame at 08:00 daily before check-in (from `event.json`), every 3 h from check-in to the end of hacking, and one the morning after. It also gets an **event-driven frame** at each priced offer's first post time and at each limit (D15), so every offer appears open on the clock at least once before it moves to Missed. Frames within the same minute collapse. An offer posted after its own limit goes straight to Missed, listed as "posted after its window closed".
- **Range:** "event week" means every captured message, the oldest of which is 2026-10-09 in the locked 25 [measured]. The first frame is the first morning after the oldest captured message, and the last frame is the morning after hacking ends. Frame cadence is in the replay semantics below.
- **Visible:** messages with `ts` ≤ D.
- **New badge:** `ts` after the previous frame and at or before this frame (D16).
- **Time limit (D15):** a credit's *limit* is its stated deadline or a claim window stated in the post or its offer group, whichever is earlier (e.g. "$50 credits at the workshop happening now", "6:00–7:00, handing out credits"). The window end is recorded in a `window_end` column in the day-2 labels and the credit sheet, filled only from the post's own text. Wherever the money clock and Missed rules below say "deadline", read "limit". Credits with neither show "offered, no stated limit".
- **Money clock at D:** the sum of the amount at D over distinct credit groups that are priced at D and where either (deadline status at D = `resolved` and that deadline > D) or (deadline status at D = `none`). Offers whose status is `unresolved` are never counted. The clock **rises when credits are posted and drops when deadlines pass**.
- **Expired at D:** offers whose resolved deadline at D is ≤ D move to a greyed "Missed" section labeled "missed on <date>". Offers with status `none` stay in their section labeled "no stated deadline". Offers with status `unresolved` stay in their section with a "deadline unclear" badge. The receipt counts everything that was offered, including missed offers.

### Capture mechanics

The content script runs on the currently open channel in the visible tab, started by the user:
- It scrolls up step by step at a human-like pace and accumulates rendered messages, de-duplicating on `ts`.
- **Grouped messages:** Slack hides the author header on consecutive posts from the same person. The script carries the last seen author header forward to those posts, including across scroll batches. When the author is ambiguous across a batch boundary, it re-scrolls until the header is rendered.
- **Author titles (D6):** `author_title` is read only if Slack renders it inline (checked in the DevTools hour). Profile cards are never opened. Only 32 of 471 top-level posters in the 54 channels have a profile title at all [measured: export], while bracket tags in display names (e.g. "[Figma]") carry the company more often.
- **Key:** each message is keyed by its `ts`, read from the row's stable attribute or permalink (confirmed in the DevTools hour), never from a DOM node or list index.
- **Accumulation:** a MutationObserver records rows as they're added, alongside an explicit scroll loop.
- **Links (OV7, required for URL merge):** anchors are serialized as `label <href>` in `text`, and the hrefs are stored in the message's `links[]`, so a labeled link like "Use this link" keeps its URL. 147 top-level posts in the locked 25 use labeled links [measured]. The DevTools hour confirms how anchors render.
- **Stop rule:** the primary stop is the rendered channel-start header. The fallback stop is 5 consecutive steps with no new keys, waiting ~1.5 s per step so lazy loading can't cause a false stop. The stop that fired is logged per channel as `stop_reason` (`channel_start` | `no_new_keys`).
- **Storage:** the content script keeps a per-channel Map and downloads `<channel_id>.json`. Nothing is held in the MV3 service worker.

Scope is the **locked 25** in `config/sponsors.json` (D11) [measured: export, 978 top-level messages, 117 of 127 credit-signal posts, 187 keyword hits, oldest top-level 2026-10-09]. They're captured first, in the file's order. **Stretch (D11):** only after all 25 are captured and saved, the other 29 sponsor channels (flagged `stretch` in sponsors.json; 10 more credit-signal posts) are captured. Stretch channels never count toward the capture-failure threshold. Capture is top-level first.

**Thread capture (day-1 stretch, D4).** Thread capture starts only after top-level capture of every channel in the set is done and saved. It covers channels whose thread replies contain credit mentions, in descending order of the export's count (cohost-anthropic 8, spons-cartesia 6, spons-bitdeer 5, spons-convex 3, spons-factory-ai 2, then ten channels with 1). It uses the same key rule. In a thread pane, the primary stop is the rendered parent message at the top of the pane, and the fallback is the same 5 × 1.5 s rule (OV9). If it lands, replies join the board and receipt with their own measured recall against the export's replies in those channels. If not, the receipt footnote states the gap.

### Reps

- **Rule:** a poster in a sponsor channel whose display name (including a bracket tag such as "[Figma]") or inline title matches that sponsor or one of its aliases in `sponsors.json`.
- **Organizers are never reps (user direction, 2026-10-27).** Excluded: display names tagged CAL HACKS / Cal Hacks, titles matching director / exec / organizer / logistics, and the organizer list derived once from the baseline's `users.json` (admins, owners, plus the tag and title matches; 55 accounts [measured: export]) into `config/organizers.json`. That file is a hand-reviewable config of names and user IDs, not message data. It's the one sanctioned use of the baseline outside the compare step, at the user's direction. The DevTools hour checks whether the DOM exposes the sender's user ID; if not, matching falls back to display name.
- **Slack Connect accounts are a rep signal.** In the DOM, a poster with Slack's external-organization badge in a sponsor channel is a rep candidate (confirm the badge selector in the DevTools hour). In the export, these show as posters missing from `users.json`: 2 accounts, in `spons-moss` and `spons-wajo-ai`.
- **No fabricated reps.** A sponsor channel with no hand-confirmed rep is listed in the Reps tab as "no rep found in captured posts", with its top-level post count. It's never filled with a guess. The list is computed from the day-3 confirmations, not hardcoded. Under the deterministic signals alone, 7 of the 23 sponsor channels in the locked 25 show no rep signal in the export: spons-apify, spons-armadin, spons-bitdeer, spons-convex, spons-notability, spons-runpod and spons-supercell [measured]. The hand confirmation decides the final list.
- **No separate 50-poster rule check.** The rule only produces candidates. On day 3, the human confirms each rep shown (`confirmed = true`) from a candidate list made of the rule's matches plus the top posters in each sponsor channel. The rule's precision never reaches the demo, so it doesn't need to be measured.
- **Opener pipeline:** the batch script picks `sample_message_id`: the rep's most recent message that carries an item, otherwise their most recent message. It generates a one-line opener referencing that post and caches it by `rep_key + sample_message_id`. This runs on day 3.
- **LinkedIn link:** a people-search URL built from the template `https://www.linkedin.com/search/results/people/?keywords=<name>%20<company>`. It's a search link only; nothing is fetched.

**Labeling, cheapest honest version** (replaces the random 150):
- **Keyword pool:** from the baseline's copy of the captured channels, top-level messages only (`thread_ts` null or equal to `ts`, D4), select messages matching a broad regex: `$`, `credit`, `code`, `promo`, `free`, `deadline`, `due`, `by <day/time>`, `tonight`, `submit`, `apply`, `RSVP`, `closes`. **Label every keyword hit, no sampling** (user direction, 2026-10-27): 187 top-level hits in the locked 25 [measured].
- **Miss checks (D12), both outside the keyword hits:** (1) hand-label every message the classifier flags as credit or dated; (2) hand-label 30 random top-level messages. Any credit or dated positive found by either is a known gap in the regex and is reported. Recall is stated as "within keyword hits, plus classifier-flagged messages outside them". The random 30 are reported separately as the estimate of what the keyword list misses.
- **Each label is quick:** is it a credit, is it dated, amount, basis, deadline, `exclude` (not an offer). It's yes/no plus fields, not free-form, using the credit sheet's column names (D13).
- **One hand pass (D13):** `hr sheet` pre-fills the human columns of `review/credits.csv` from the day-2 label for single-credit messages. The day-4 pass only assigns `offer_key`, reviews unlabeled and multi-item rows, and resolves rows where the label and the classifier disagree. Labels stay the gate's ground truth and never feed the classifier.
- **Minimums:** ≥ 20 credit and ≥ 20 dated positives. Labeling every keyword hit makes this a census of the pool, so any shortfall is the real count of the captured channels, and it's stated with the gate numbers. Field accuracy is reported with its n, since only a handful of priced credits exist [measured: 40 `$<digit>` posts in the locked 25].

**Day-2 gate (all on the labeled set):**
- **Capture recall:** ≥ 95% of the labeled credit and dated messages from the baseline are present in the extension capture.
- **Classification:** precision ≥ 85% and recall ≥ 85% on credit and dated items.
- **Field accuracy:** exact `amount_usd_per_person` (including correctly marking items unpriced) on ≥ 90% of labeled priced credits, and `deadline_iso` correct to the day on ≥ 90% of labeled dated items (to the minute where a time is stated).
- Report the positive counts next to every percentage.

**Day-1 per-channel misses** (handled on the morning of day 2, at most 2 hours):
- **Keyed recall < 90%:** fix the script and re-run that channel. If it still fails, drop the channel and list it on screen as "not captured".
- **Author match < 95%:** that channel's posters skip the reps rule and go through the hand-built allowlist. Captured messages are still used for items.
- **Time > 2 min:** recorded, but not a failure on its own.

**What counts as a capture failure** (this triggers the capture fallback): after the drops above, either day-2 capture recall is < 95%, or fewer than 60% of the locked 25 (15) are still captured (D11; stretch channels don't count).

**Fallbacks by failure mode:**
- **Capture fails** (as defined above): ship A + reps on the partial extension capture, with the measured loss shown on screen. Cut the receipt and money clock. slackdump stays out of the product.
- **Classification or field accuracy fails (D14):** board cards that have no hand check get an "unverified" label on their amounts and dates. The receipt and money clock still ship, because every input to them is hand-checked in the credit sheet. That sheet holds the classifier's credit items plus every labeled credit the classifier missed. Only a capture failure cuts the receipt.
- **Reps rule fails:** use the hand-built allowlist (no scope cut).

**Receipt wording** always names its basis: "Found in C captured channels", where C is computed from the final `runs` record after any drops, never hardcoded. If any channel was dropped, the receipt adds "of N attempted" (N from the runs record: 25, plus any stretch channels attempted) and names the channels not captured.
